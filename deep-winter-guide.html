What is Deep Winter?

Deep Winter sits between Cool Winter and Deep Autumn on the seasonal flow chart. This season combines Winter's coolness with Autumn's depth, creating a rich, intense appearance. Deep Winter is sometimes called Dark Winter.

If you're a Deep Winter, your primary characteristic is depth - your overall coloring is dark and rich. Your secondary aspect is coolness, which distinguishes you from Deep Autumn. You have high contrast but the overall effect is one of intensity and richness rather than icy clarity.

When you look in the mirror, you notice depth first - dark hair, deep-toned skin, or richly colored eyes. Your coloring has weight and substance. You need saturated, deep colors that match your naturally intense appearance.

Your Characteristics

Eyes

Deep Winter eyes are dark and intense. They typically come in deep brown, dark hazel, or very deep blue-green - always with cool undertones and significant depth. There's a richness to the eye color, and the contrast between your iris and the white of your eye creates that signature striking look.

Skin

Deep Winter skin ranges widely but always has cool undertones. You might have fair skin that tans easily to olive, medium olive skin, or deep brown skin - but always with cool, blue, or neutral undertones. Silver jewelry flatters you more than gold. Your skin has no golden, peachy, or warm cast. Deep Winters often have dark features that create overall depth.

Hair

Deep Winter hair is typically very dark - black, dark brown with cool undertones, or salt-and-pepper with depth. Your hair is rich and saturated in color, not light or mousy. There are no warm red or golden tones - any highlights or dimension come from cool, ashy, or blue-black tones.

Contrast

Deep Winter features high contrast, but it comes from depth rather than lightness. Even if you have darker skin, there's contrast between your features - perhaps very dark hair and eyes against deep olive skin, or the intensity of your eyes. The overall effect is rich and dramatic rather than icy and crisp.

Deep Winter vs Deep Autumn

Deep Winters and Deep Autumns both have deep, rich coloring with high contrast. The key difference is undertone: Deep Winter is cool, while Deep Autumn is warm. This changes which deep colors harmonize with your natural coloring.

Deep Winter

  • Cool undertones (blue-based)
  • Best in icy accents and jewel tones
  • Silver jewelry flatters more
  • Magenta, royal purple, sapphire suit best
  • Can wear some icy pastels

Deep Autumn

  • Warm undertones (golden-based)
  • Best in rich earth tones
  • Gold jewelry flatters more
  • Rust, forest green, burgundy suit best
  • Warm, spicy colors work well

Try holding magenta and rust orange near your face. If magenta makes you look vibrant and rust looks muddy, you're a Deep Winter. If rust looks rich and magenta clashes, you're a Deep Autumn.
