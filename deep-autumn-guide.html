What is Deep Autumn?

Deep Autumn is one of the three Autumn seasons and sits between Deep Winter and True Autumn on the seasonal flow chart. This season combines intense depth with rich warmth, creating a dramatic, luxurious appearance.

If you're a Deep Autumn, the primary characteristic of your overall appearance is depth, with warmth as the secondary aspect. This means deep, rich colors suit you best, and they should have warm undertones to harmonize with your coloring.

When you look in the mirror, you notice the overall intensity and depth of your coloring - there's richness and darkness to your features. Your eyes, skin, and hair all have depth, creating a striking, luxurious appearance with warm undertones.

Your Characteristics

Eyes

Deep Autumn eyes are dark and intense. They typically come in deep brown, dark hazel, deep green, or dark amber. Your eyes have significant depth and warmth, with rich, dark coloring that creates a striking appearance. The whites of your eyes provide contrast against your dark irises, enhancing the intensity of your gaze.

Skin

Deep Autumn skin ranges from medium olive to deep bronze or ebony and always has warm undertones. Your skin has a rich, golden-warm quality - whether you're light, medium, or deep-toned. Gold jewelry is dramatically more flattering than silver. Many Deep Autumns can tan deeply and have a naturally warm glow. Your skin tone has depth and richness.

Hair

True to the Deep Autumn depth, your hair is naturally dark. It ranges from dark auburn, deep chestnut, rich brown, to black-brown with warm undertones. Whatever the shade, your hair is dark and may have warm, copper, or red highlights when caught in the light. The overall effect is rich and luxurious.

Contrast

Deep Autumn is characterized by medium to high contrast between skin, hair, and eyes. Your dark hair and eyes create drama against your skin, or all your features may be similarly deep and rich. The overall effect is intense and striking.

Deep Autumn vs Deep Winter

Deep Autumns are often confused with Deep Winters since both seasons share depth as their primary characteristic and both have high contrast. The key difference lies in their secondary aspect: Deep Autumn is warm, while Deep Winter is cool.

Deep Autumn

  • Warm undertones (golden, bronze)
  • Rich, earthy quality
  • Deep warm tones
  • Best in burgundy, rust, olive
  • Gold jewelry is more flattering

Deep Winter

  • Cool undertones (blue, rosy)
  • Clear, jewel-like quality
  • Deep cool tones
  • Best in navy, magenta, emerald
  • Silver jewelry is more flattering

If you're still unsure, compare burgundy (Deep Autumn) and magenta (Deep Winter) against your face. Deep Autumns look rich and luxurious in warm burgundy, while cool magenta can look harsh. Deep Winters shine in jewel-toned magenta but look dull in earthy burgundy.
